# Consented usability study — protocol only, no participants enrolled

Status: NOT RUN. Do not present test records, automation runs, or developer QA as real-user feedback.

Target: 5–8 consenting adults familiar with ML monitoring or delivery operations. Use a separate study environment and fictitious orders; never request customer names, addresses, private order histories, credentials, or payment data. The current deployment is owner-private; external recruitment and participant access have not been arranged.

Before participation, explain: this is a portfolio research prototype, participation is voluntary, tasks take about 15 minutes, responses are used to improve the interface, no recording occurs by default, and they can stop or omit any response. Obtain explicit consent before collecting responses. Assign a random participant code. Agree a deletion/contact route and retention period before starting; these must reflect the operator's actual ability to honor them.

Tasks: (1) score a fictitious order; (2) explain why an unresolved outcome is excluded from observed accuracy; (3) record a delivery and interpret the new bounds; (4) explain why one correct result does not establish 100% general accuracy; (5) distinguish collecting evidence from confirmed harm; (6) find a documented research failure.

Record only participant code, consent version/date, task completion, task time, errors, confidence rating 1–5, and optional non-identifying comments. Report all recruited participants and withdrawals. Suggested success criteria fixed before recruitment: at least 80% unassisted completion of tasks 1–3; at least 80% correct interpretation of task 4; zero interface claims of validated fairness or automatic model safety. These are usability criteria, not model-performance tests.

No invitations have been sent, no responses have been invented, and no public-deployment or user-study completion claim is justified yet.
