RESEARCH EVIDENCE · 19 NOVEMBER 2026 · SYNTHETIC ADAPTATION STUDY

Detect harm. Then earn the model change.

A fixed-cohort detector preserves missing outcomes in its denominator. A prospective shadow model must demonstrate improvement on later predictions before promotion. These experiments show both the benefits and the remaining failures.

A result with boundaries

With eight minority predictions per day and severe drift plus slower reporting, the prospective policy reduced average minority error from 62.6% to 40.1%, with one refit per run. Stable and reporting-only scenarios caused no alarms or refits in these 100 runs per scenario. This is a synthetic result, not a production accuracy or fairness claim.

At two minority predictions per day, only 38 of 100 severe-drift runs promoted a candidate. With 30-day delayed labels, the same eight-per-day setting detected none of 100 harmful streams. Missing feedback can make the system abstain indefinitely.

What was tested

  • Initial seeds 5000–5099: six policies, nine scenarios, three group sizes. The first gate exhausted its budget too early and failed to adapt.
  • Follow-up seeds 6000–6099: the same six policies plus a prospective shadow policy. All parameters were fixed before this follow-up; the failed initial results are retained.
  • Majority: 80 predictions/day. Minority: 2, 8 or 24. Monitoring lasts 120 days, with 30 reference days and drift beginning on day 40.
  • Severe drift changes error from 0.12 to 0.88; mild drift changes it to 0.35. Normal reporting is after 1 or 9 days. Stress cases include 30-day delays and permanently missing labels.
  • Candidate classifiers are identity/inversion by group. They are not the logistic model serving real delivery predictions.

The decision rules

For n predictions, e observed errors and u pending outcomes, the complete empirical error lies between e/n and (e+u)/n. We widen this range using a Hoeffding radius, allocating a finite error budget across groups and scheduled looks. A harm alarm requires the current lower bound to exceed the reference upper bound by five percentage points.

The candidate is frozen before its prospective validation cohort. Every pending paired outcome receives the worst possible improvement, −1. Promotion requires the lower improvement bound to exceed two percentage points. Other groups' synthetic models remain unchanged.

The bound assumes independent bounded losses and fixed cohorts. It is not valid without qualification under temporal dependence, adaptive cohort selection, or repeated horizon resets. The operational dashboard uses 27 states and a later day-67 start; its power is not measured by this two-group experiment.

Related work and remaining evidence

Sequential inference, hierarchical verification and staged adaptation have substantial prior work: Howard et al., Request-and-Reverify, and DriftSurf. We do not claim to have invented these ideas or reproduced their algorithms.

Outstanding: strong external detector baselines, real-stream adaptation evaluation, dependence and unseen-delay stress tests, independent novelty/patent review, public participant access and a consented user study.
